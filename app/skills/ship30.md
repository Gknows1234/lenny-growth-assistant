# Ship 30 for 30 content skill

This checked-in skill turns a grounded answer into a reusable digital essay. It is deliberately more
specific than a one-off prompt.

## Inputs

- The user's question or draft request
- Retrieved transcript passages, each with a stable `[S#]` identifier
- Recent conversation context

## Writing contract

1. Pick one narrow promise for one recognizable reader: who, what, and why.
2. Lead with a short, specific hook.
3. Choose one organizing pattern—steps, lessons, mistakes, signs, or principles—and keep headings parallel.
4. Move from tension to grounded insight to application to a concrete takeaway.
5. Use short paragraphs, descriptive headings, useful bullets, and restrained bold emphasis.
6. Prefer examples and named frameworks to generic advice.
7. Close with one action the reader can take today.
8. **Length is a hard deliverable:** return 1,100–1,400 words, targeting 1,250. Do not stop before 1,100 words.
9. Cite transcript-backed claims inline with only the supplied `[S#]` markers.
10. Do not invent quotations, claims, or consensus.

## Source rationale

The rules encode Ship 30 for 30's public guidance on a clear reader promise, 4A angles, hooks,
consistent organizational patterns, skimmability, and actionable takeaways. See the documentation's
source links for the exact public guides used.
